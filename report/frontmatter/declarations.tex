\section*{Use of generative AI}

Generative AI assistants were used in three capacities: as a coding assistant, for the
simulator, sweep scripts and regression tests of Chapter~\ref{ch:method}; as a writing and
editing aid, in drafting, restructuring and proofreading the prose; and as an adversarial
reviewer, checking claims against primary sources, the prose against the simulator's actual
behaviour, and each chapter as an unfamiliar examiner would read it.

All such use was under my direction and subject to my review, and its output was treated as
a proposal to be checked rather than a result to be accepted. Several errors in earlier
drafts were found that way and are acknowledged where they occur, and one conclusion was
withdrawn when its reasoning did not survive scrutiny. The research question, the choice of
protocols, the experimental design, the interpretation of the results and the conclusions
are my own. I have read and verified every paragraph of this report, I take full
responsibility for its entire contents, and every reference has been checked against its
primary source.

\section*{Ethical considerations}

This project involved no human participants and gathered no personal data. It required no
ethics approval.

It nonetheless analyses mechanisms in live financial venues, and the boundary held is stated
here as well as in Section~\ref{sec:ethics-intro}. No real capital was committed at any
point. No transaction was signed on any network, mainnet or testnet. No request of any kind
was sent to Sapience's auction relayer, which distributes requests to real market makers, so
no synthetic order flow entered a live venue. All protocol facts were established by reading
published documentation and open-source implementations, and every empirical result comes
from a simulator.

The work is dual-use, and saying so is part of holding the boundary. An analysis of what a
mechanism discloses is also a description of what an observer could learn from it.
Chapter~\ref{ch:protocols} therefore confines itself to design that the protocols themselves
publish, names no specific market, participant or position, and presents mitigations
alongside the analysis rather than deferring them. What the report contributes on this
surface is a measurement of what disclosure costs and an argument about how that cost should
be classified; it offers no method for capturing it, and
Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} reaches no verdict on the classification. The ethical position
does not depend on that verdict going either way. Section~\ref{sec:ethics-intro} sets out the boundary and the reasons it is
defensible, and Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} what the analysis can and cannot claim.

\section*{Sustainability}

The computational cost of this project was small and is stated rather than estimated
loosely. The complete experiment consists of six parameter sweeps at $200{,}000$ draws per
cell, and the full pipeline that regenerates every number and every figure in this report
runs in approximately five seconds on a single laptop CPU. No model training was performed,
no GPU was used, and no inference against a hosted language model forms part of the
experimental results. The generative AI assistance described above carries an energy cost
that I have not attempted to quantify, and which is likely to exceed the cost of the
experiment itself.

The project deliberately avoided approaches with materially higher cost. The specification
sensitivity experiment described in Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions} as future work would have
required several hundred language model inferences; it was cut for reasons of time rather
than energy, but the saving is real and worth recording.

\section*{Availability of data and materials}

All code, data and the source of this report are available at
\url{https://github.com/arsenzholanov/msc-project-kit}. The repository is private; read
access has been granted to the supervisor and the second marker. The
repository contains the simulator, the sweep and figure scripts, the regression tests, the
raw sweep outputs, the LaTeX source of this report, and the working notes recording which
protocol pages were consulted and on what dates. Its commit history records how the work
developed.

Every figure and every number in Chapters~\ref{ch:results} and~\ref{ch:evaluation} is
regenerable from the repository with two commands, from a fixed seed; the sweep outputs
committed to the repository are reproduced byte for byte by rerunning it. No data used in
this project is confidential or subject to access restriction, and no third-party data was
used.
